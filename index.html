How SpectraHunt spots something that moves

Every field is compared with itself across visits. Stars stay put. Anything else shows up in the difference.

  1. 1

    Align the passes

    Each visit to a field is registered to a common pixel grid and background-subtracted.

  2. 2

    Subtract one from the other

    Stationary stars cancel out. A mover leaves a paired bright and dark spot: where it is, and where it was.

  3. 3

    Check the catalogs

    Every candidate is cross-matched against SkyBoT's known solar-system objects. Anything without a match goes to a person for review.

What SPHEREx is watching for

SPHEREx (Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer) is a NASA all-sky survey observatory, imaging the entire sky roughly every six months across 102 near-infrared bands from 0.75 to 5 microns. Because it revisits the same sky repeatedly, anything that moves, brightens, or fades between visits stands out once you compare epochs directly: comets, asteroids, variable stars, and more.

How detection will work

Each sky cell with two or more epochs gets aligned to a common grid, background-subtracted, and differenced. Whatever's left over after masking bad pixels gets run through source extraction. Real movers show up as a paired bright and dark dipole; noise and cosmic rays don't pair up the same way. Every candidate is cross-checked against SkyBoT's catalog of known solar-system objects before it reaches the feed.
