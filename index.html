About Trace

This is a design mockup with generated placeholder data — no live SPHEREx imagery is being fetched. It exists to show what we're proposing to build: an interface anyone can use to watch the SPHEREx sky change over time.

What SPHEREx is watching for

SPHEREx (Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer) is a NASA all-sky survey observatory, imaging the entire sky roughly every six months across 102 near-infrared bands from 0.75 to 5 microns. Because it revisits the same sky repeatedly, anything that moves, brightens, or fades between visits stands out once you compare epochs directly — comets, asteroids, variable stars, and more.

How detection will work

Each sky cell with two or more epochs gets aligned to a common grid, background-subtracted, and differenced. Whatever's left over — after masking bad pixels — gets run through source extraction. Real movers show up as a paired bright/dark "dipole"; noise and cosmic rays don't pair up the same way. Every candidate is cross-checked against SkyBoT's catalog of known solar-system objects before it reaches the feed.
