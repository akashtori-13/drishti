# Demo walkthrough (90 seconds)

Goal: a first-time visitor finds and watches a moving object in under 60 seconds.

## Script

1. **Land (0:00–0:10)** — Open the app. The hero sky twinkles behind the headline
   "Hunt what moves in the infrared sky". The first-visit tutorial card offers 4
   steps; press "Start hunting".
2. **Search (0:10–0:25)** — Type `3I/ATLAS` (resolves via Sesame) or press
   **Surprise me**. Click the **3I/ATLAS** chip. You land in the blink view:
   Aug 2025 vs Dec 2025, same D3 band.
3. **Scrub (0:25–0:45)** — Drag the **timeline slider**. Press **Play**.
   Epoch tags show the date; the readout shows RA/Dec, obs ID, MJD/UTC.
4. **Difference (0:45–1:05)** — Switch mode to **Difference**. Stationary stars
   cancel to gray; the comet remnant pops. Flip on **Motion color**: epoch B is
   red, epoch A is green. Toggle **Movers** to draw the A→B motion vector.
5. **Inspect + share (1:05–1:30)** — Read measured motion (″/day), the light
   curve, and cross-match links (SkyBoT, MPC checker, Horizons, SIMBAD).
   Click **Copy share link**: the URL encodes showcase, band, epoch, and mode
   (`#/blink/3i-atlas?band=3&epoch=1&mode=diff`). Paste it in a fresh tab —
   the same view restores. Export **Candidates CSV**.

## What to say

> "SPHEREx photographs the entire sky every six months in 102 infrared colors.
> SpectraHunt lines up each visit so anyone can blink between them. Stars stay
> put; anything that moves or flares stands out in the difference. Every pixel
> traces to NASA/IRSA — links to the original FITS are in the metadata panel."
